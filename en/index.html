Frequently asked questions

About finding Latvian ancestors and old photographs

How do I find a photograph of my grandparents or great-grandparents if nothing has survived in the family?

Almost every adult who lived in Latvia between 1919 and 1940 had a passport with a photo glued in. Many of these passport files have survived — you need to know where to look and put in the work to find them. Beyond that there are school, University of Latvia, army and Aizsargi files, the press and regional museums.

What else can you find out about a person?

Whether or not a photograph is available, we can often also establish where and when the person was born, where they lived, their parents’ names, brothers and sisters, children, and what they did for a living.

How far back can ancestors be found?

Photographs — realistically from 1880 to 1950. Documents (birth, marriage and death records) can often be traced back to the 18th century, but without faces.

What if nothing is found?

You pay nothing. Within a week we tell you whether a photo exists, and if we don’t find one, the search costs you nothing.

Do you also search in Latgale, Estonia, Lithuania and Russia?

Yes — Latgale’s congregations and parishes are held in Latvian archives. Estonian and Lithuanian archives are available online. Russian archives are currently restricted; we assess each case individually.

Do you work with Latvians abroad?

Yes — many of our clients are descendants of Latvians in the USA, Canada, Australia, the UK and Germany. We reply in English, invoice in euros or dollars, and send the result digitally or by post.
